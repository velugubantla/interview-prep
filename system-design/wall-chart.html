Twenty minutes before the round

  1. Scope before you design. Get explicit agreement.
  2. Say a number in the first eight minutes.
  3. Draw the happy path end to end before optimizing anything.
  4. Name the alternative and why you rejected it — before being asked.
  5. Offer the deep dive: "the two risky parts are X and Y — which?"
  6. Say what breaks first and how you would detect it.
  7. Say "I don't know," then reason from first principles.
  8. Stop talking. Check in. It is a collaboration, not a lecture.
  9. Don't over-engineer. "One primary is fine here" is a strong-hire sentence.
  10. Close by recapping the two or three decisions that defined the design.
