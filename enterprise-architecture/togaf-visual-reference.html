The four boundaries

Learn these and most of the 14 ADM questions fall out.

Preliminary  |  A
PreliminarySets up the capability. Who does architecture, by what method, under what principles.
Phase ASets up the project. What's in scope, who the stakeholders are, and formal permission to start.
D  |  E
Phases B, C, DProduce gaps. Each domain compares baseline to target and records what's missing.
Phase EConsumes gaps. Consolidates them into work packages, projects and Transition Architectures.
E  |  F
Phase EDecides what gets built and in what increments.
Phase FDecides when, in what order, and at what cost. Cost/benefit and risk live here.
G  |  H
Phase GEnforces the architecture on delivery. Contract signed, compliance reviewed.
Phase HChanges the architecture itself. Triages change requests into maintenance, incremental change, or a new cycle.

Terms that look alike

This is the category where most marks are lost. Read the right-hand column as the discriminator.

Deliverable · Artifact · Building Block
DeliverableContractually specified. Formally reviewed and signed off. The output of a phase.
ArtifactA granular description of one aspect. Catalog, matrix or diagram. Usually packaged inside a deliverable.
Building BlockA reusable component of capability. Can appear in many deliverables and many architectures.
Architecture Building Block · Solution Building Block
ABBCapability-level. Implementation-independent. Says what is required and shapes the SBB specification.
SBBThe actual product, component or service that realizes the capability. Vendor-specific.
View · Viewpoint
ViewpointWhere you stand and what you look at. The conventions and perspective. Chosen to address a concern.
ViewWhat you actually see from there. The representation shown to the stakeholder.
Architecture Definition Document · Architecture Requirements Specification
Definition DocumentQualitative. Narrative. Describes what the architecture is.
Requirements SpecificationQuantitative. Measurable. States what any implementation must satisfy.
Architecture Continuum · Solutions Continuum
Architecture ContinuumThe generic-to-specific progression of architectures: what should be built.
Solutions ContinuumThe corresponding implementations at each level: what actually was built.
Baseline · Target · Transition
BaselineWhere you are now.
TargetWhere you're going.
TransitionA formally described state in between that delivers business value in its own right — not just a technical waypoint.

Architecture Landscape — three altitudes

Breadth decreases, detail increases, as you go down.

Strategic ArchitectureLong-term summary view of the entire enterprise. Direction-setting. Low detail, maximum breadth.
Segment ArchitectureDetailed, formal description of a portion of the enterprise. Used to align change activity within that portion.
Capability ArchitectureDetailed view of a specific increment of capability. Narrow scope, highest detail, closest to delivery.

The compliance ladder

Read each rung as two facts: was everything in the spec built, and was anything extra built?

Fully Conformant — exact correspondence. Everything in the spec built, nothing extra.
Conformant — everything in the spec built to spec, plus extra features not in the spec.
Compliant — not everything in the spec was built, but everything built follows the spec. A clean subset.
Consistent — some spec features built to spec, some spec features missing, and extra features added.
Irrelevant — no features in common with the spec at all.
Non-Conformant — any case where something was built not in accordance with the spec.

The trap: Compliant sounds stronger than Conformant in ordinary English. In TOGAF it is weaker. Conformant means the spec was fully delivered; Compliant means only part of it was.

The techniques toolkit

Each one exists to solve a specific problem. The exam tests what problem, not how to run it.

Architecture Principles Preliminary
General rules that constrain every later decision.
Stakeholder Management Phase A
Identify who matters, what they care about, and which views they need.
Business Scenarios Phase A / B
Derive requirements from a described process, its actors and its desired outcome.
Gap Analysis B, C, D, E
Find what was left out between baseline and target.
Architecture Patterns Any
Reuse a proven solution to a recurring problem in context.
Interoperability Requirements Any
Define what has to work with what, and to what degree.
Business Transformation Readiness A / E
Assess whether the organization can absorb the change at all.
Risk Management Throughout
Classify by effect and frequency, before mitigation (initial) and after (residual).
Capability-Based Planning Throughout
Plan outcomes as business capabilities, delivered horizontally across silos.
Migration Planning Techniques E / F
Sequence and prioritize work packages into a deliverable plan.

Anatomy of an architecture principle

Four parts, always. The fourth is what makes it enforceable rather than decorative.

NameMemorable, and it says the rule — not the mechanism.
StatementThe rule itself, unambiguous.
RationaleThe business benefit. Why this rule exists.
ImplicationsWhat it costs and what it requires. The teeth.
